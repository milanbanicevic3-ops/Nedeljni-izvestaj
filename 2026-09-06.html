NFP Shocks Both Ways — US +162k, Canada -41.7k
◆ Delta Box — What Changed Since the 09-04 Friday Brief
Friday's NFP delivered a genuine shock, and it was decisive in the direction most sources weren't leaning: US payrolls surged 162,000 against a roughly +56,000 consensus, unemployment held at 4.1%, and wage growth came in at 3.1% y/y. This reinforced rather than broke the hawkish regime this desk has tracked for two weeks — September Fed hike odds moved to roughly 62-65% from around 55% pre-release.
The same release delivered an equally sharp shock in the opposite direction for Canada: employment fell 41,700 against a roughly +15,000 consensus, with wage growth cooling to 2% y/y. That divergence — US labor strength against a genuine Canadian labor shock — is now this week's single most consistently repeated, highest-conviction trade idea: long USD/CAD, a clean reversal from the short-USD/CAD bias that dominated most of August.
Gold fell hard on the jobs shock, closing near $4,328.89 and breaking back below both the 200-day moving average and the $4,400 level. CFTC data (report date Sept 1, so predating Friday's move) already showed speculative net longs pulling back for the first time in weeks, down roughly 15,200 contracts — positioning was already softening before the jobs data delivered the actual catalyst.
USD/JPY closed Friday near 156.52, still well off the intervention-triggering levels above 160 from two weeks ago, but the underlying dynamic hasn't changed: Japan has confirmed spending ¥15.4 trillion defending the yen, and 160.000 remains a direct, live intervention-risk zone that multiple sources continue to flag as an explicit avoid for outright longs.
The week ahead is genuinely built around a single resolution point: Friday's US CPI. One source states this directly — the primary risk to this week's firmer-USD regime is a softer CPI print that reverses the NFP-driven dollar bid and forces rapid repositioning. Thursday's ECB decision (a 25bp hike is the market's working assumption, though worth flagging that this specific expectation could not be independently verified within one source's own retrieved data) adds a second, real catalyst two days earlier.

Regime

Friday's jobs report reinforced the hawkish regime rather than breaking it, and it did so through a genuinely clean, two-sided surprise rather than a single data point. US payrolls beat by roughly 106,000 jobs against consensus — a large, unambiguous surprise, not a marginal one. Canada's employment fell 41,700 against an expected gain of roughly 15,000, a genuine labor-market shock in the opposite direction. That combination is why long USD/CAD has become this week's single most consistently repeated, highest-conviction idea across source material, replacing the short-USD/CAD bias that dominated most of August.

Gold's reaction confirms the jobs data landed as a real catalyst, not noise. The metal broke back below both its 200-day moving average and the $4,400 level, and CFTC positioning data (predating Friday's move) already showed speculative longs pulling back for the first time in weeks — a sign the market was already de-risking before the jobs shock gave it a clean reason to extend that move.

Everything this week builds toward Friday's US CPI, and one source names this directly as the primary risk to the current regime rather than burying it in a list of equal-weight events. A soft CPI print would reverse the NFP-driven dollar bid and force rapid repositioning across essentially every pair in this brief; a hot or sticky print would entrench the current regime further ahead of the September 15-16 FOMC meeting. Thursday's ECB decision, with a 25bp hike as the market's working assumption, adds a second real catalyst two days earlier — though worth flagging honestly that this specific hike expectation could not be independently verified within one source's own retrieved data this week, so treat it as consensus positioning rather than confirmed fact.

Central bank stances
Fed

Holds 3.50-3.75%, increasingly hawkish after Friday's jobs beat; September hike odds near 62-65%. The FOMC meets September 15-16, after this week's CPI and ECB decision both land — meaning the Committee will have the freshest possible inflation read in hand by the time it decides.

ECB

Decision Thursday, September 10; a 25bp hike is the market's working assumption amid residual inflation pressure, though this specific expectation is presented as consensus positioning rather than a confirmed, independently-verified fact this week. A genuine surprise here — a hold instead of a hike, or a larger move — would be a real, named event risk in its own right, separate from Friday's CPI.

BoJ

Holds at 1.00%, continuing gradual normalization with further hikes priced. Japan's confirmed ¥15.4 trillion intervention spend remains the dominant fact for USD/JPY; 160.000 stays a direct, live risk zone regardless of how the week's US data resolves.

RBA / RBNZ / BoC

RBA remains the highest policy rate in G10 at 4.35% with a hawkish bias, the clearest domestic-rate story in the majors right now. RBNZ hiked 25bp to 2.75% on September 2 (full committee consensus, no dissent) with explicit guidance the OCR may rise gradually toward roughly 3.2% over two years, though near-term urgency looks limited per the actual policy statement. BoC holds 2.25%; Friday's Canadian jobs shock weakens the case for continued patience, and the framework itself explicitly allows using the 1-3% inflation control range flexibly to pursue maximum sustainable employment when conditions warrant — worth watching whether that flexibility gets invoked given this week's data.

Friday's CPI is the single most likely catalyst to produce that combination this week, if it's going to happen at all.
